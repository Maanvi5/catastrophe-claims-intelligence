# Problem Discovery

## 1. Initial Observation

A major weather event can generate a large volume of insurance claims over a short period of time. This creates a claims-management challenge: the insurer needs to determine how to allocate limited claims capacity and decide which claims require faster handling, adjuster assessment, or deeper investigation.

## 2. Initial Problem Hypothesis

When a weather catastrophe generates a large volume of claims, claims teams need to evaluate and route claims into the appropriate handling path.

This is an initial hypothesis and has not yet been validated against the current industry workflow.

## 3. Initial Assumptions About Claim Information

From a first-principles perspective, potentially relevant information may include:

- Claim amount
- Asset / property type
- Location
- Severity of the reported damage
- Severity of the weather event

These are working assumptions, not yet confirmed as the actual information used by claims teams.

## 4. Emerging Question: When Does the Problem Begin?

An important observation is that catastrophe claims management may begin before individual claims are reported.

Once a significant weather event occurs—or becomes highly likely—an insurer may already have an estimate of potential exposure and future claims.

This raises two potential decision points:

### Before / during the event

What should the insurer expect and prepare for?

Potential questions include:
- What part of the insured portfolio may be affected?
- How many claims might emerge?
- How severe could losses be?
- What claims capacity may be required?

### After the event

What actually happened, and how should incoming claims be handled?

Potential questions include:
- Which assets were affected?
- Which claims require faster handling?
- Which claims need an adjuster?
- Which claims require deeper assessment?

## 5. Environmental Intelligence Hypothesis

Environmental information may potentially provide useful evidence at one or more points in this workflow, including:

- Weather forecasts
- Observed weather
- Event severity
- Flood / hazard extent
- Satellite or aerial imagery

However, insurers already use environmental and catastrophe information in parts of this workflow. The objective of discovery is therefore not to assume that environmental intelligence is missing, but to identify whether there is an important unmet need.

## 6. Key Research Questions

1. How does catastrophe claims management actually work today?
2. What decisions are made before, during and after an event?
3. Who makes each decision?
4. What information and systems do they use?
5. Where is environmental intelligence already used?
6. What limitations or gaps remain?
7. Which decision, if any, presents a meaningful product opportunity?
